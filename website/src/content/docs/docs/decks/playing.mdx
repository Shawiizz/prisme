Each deck is one band across the window: its panel on the left, the waveform in the middle, the
tempo fader at the end where a turntable has it. Under them, a rail holds the transport, the
stems, **SYNC**, the pitch and the pads. This page covers what a deck does when you play it by
itself, without a record. With a timecode record, the turntable drives the deck instead: see
[Vinyl control modes](/docs/dvs/modes/).

![Two decks: the panel with the remaining time, BPM and key on the left, the waveforms, and under each the CUE and PLAY keys, the stems, SYNC and the pads](../../../../assets/screens/decks.png)

## Load a track

You can put a track on a deck in several ways:

| How | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| Drag a track from the library onto a deck | That deck (the deck is outlined while you drag over it) |
| Click **A** or **B** in the **LOAD** column of a track | That deck |
| **Load on deck A** in a track's menu (right click) | That deck |
| Double-click a track | The first empty deck, or deck A if none is empty |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> / <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> | The selected track on deck A / deck B |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | The selected track on the first empty deck, or deck A |
| In the [command palette](/docs/interface/keyboard/), <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> / <kbd>Alt</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> on a track | Deck A / deck B |

The deck shows **Loading…** while the file is read, then its cover, title and artist. A new
track always starts at its beginning, stopped, and the deck forgets the previous track's loop and
**SYNC**. Its tempo fader, **KEY LOCK** and **SLIP** stay as you set them.

The waveform of a track loaded before is there at once. A track loaded for the first time draws
its waveform from its start as the file is read, the rest filling in within about a second,
while it can already play.

If the deck is playing, Prisme asks before it replaces the track: **Deck A is playing**, with
**Load** and **Cancel**. To make a playing deck untouchable during a set, turn on
**Never replace a playing deck** in **Settings** › **DJ**: a load onto a playing deck is then
refused with a message instead of asked about.

<Aside type="tip">
**Headphones on the deck a track is loaded on** (**Settings** › **DJ**) puts the deck you just
loaded alone in the headphones, ready to cue. See [Headphones](/docs/mixer/headphones/).
</Aside>

The BPM and key appear once the track is analysed. A track analysed for the first time when you
load it shows **—** for a moment; see [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/).

## Play, pause and cue

The rail starts with **CUE** and **PLAY**. **PLAY** starts the deck and becomes **PAUSE** while
it plays. **CUE** works as on a CDJ:

| When you press CUE | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The deck is playing | It goes back to the cue point and stops |
| The deck is stopped on the cue point | It plays while you hold **CUE**, and goes back to the cue point when you let go, unless you pressed **PLAY** meanwhile |
| The deck is stopped anywhere else | The cue point moves there |

To set a cue point and start the track from it:

<Steps>

1. Press **PLAY** and let the track run to the bar you want to start from, then press **PAUSE**.
   You can also get there along the overview.
2. Press **CUE**: the cue point is set there.
3. Hold **CUE** to hear the start; let go and the deck goes back to the cue point.
4. When it is time, press **PLAY**.

</Steps>

With quantize on, the cue point lands on the nearest beat and the deck moves onto it. A track
without a cue point cues from its very start. The cue point is saved with the track in your
library, and shows on the waveform as a flag marked **C**. More in
[Cues and hot cues](/docs/decks/cues/).

| Key | Deck A | Deck B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CUE** (hold to preview) | <kbd>A</kbd> | <kbd>J</kbd> |
| **PLAY** / **PAUSE** | <kbd>S</kbd> | <kbd>K</kbd> |

Keys are named as on a US QWERTY keyboard; Prisme reads their position, so on another layout use
the key in the same place. Decks C and D take the keys of A and B with <kbd>Shift</kbd> held. The
full list, and how to change a key, is in [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/interface/keyboard/).

<Aside type="tip">
To start the track from the cue point without a gap, hold **CUE**, press **PLAY** while you hold
it, then let go of **CUE**: the deck carries on playing, as on a CDJ.
</Aside>

## Move around the track

The waveform has two parts: the scrolling view around the playhead, and the overview of the
whole track under it.

- **Click or drag along the overview** to go anywhere in the track: the deck follows your
  pointer as a record under a hand.
- **Slide the scrolling view** at once to nudge the track, as the edge of a jog wheel: a playing
  track is pushed ahead or held back for a moment, a stopped one moves.
- **Press and hold still** on the scrolling view for a moment, then move: your hand is on the
  record, and the deck plays through your motion like a scratch. Let go and it plays on.

A click or a tap on the scrolling view does nothing, nor does a press held still: only a slide
moves the track, so a hand or a finger resting on it by mistake cannot move it.

## The readouts

Three readouts sit in each deck's panel, big enough to read from a step back.

**REMAINING** is the time left, `−2:15.7`, with the elapsed time and the length under it
(`1:23.4 of 3:39.1`). While the deck plays, it turns amber for the last 30 seconds, then red and
blinking for the last 10.

**BPM** is the tempo you hear: the track's tempo times the pitch. Under it, the track's own tempo
and the pitch applied to it (`124.00 +1.20 %`). A dot after the **BPM** label (**BPM •**) tells
you the beat grid was corrected by hand.

**KEY** is the key you hear, as a Camelot code in the colour of its place on the wheel, with its
name under it (`8A`, `A minor`). Without **KEY LOCK**, the pitch moves the key with the tempo,
and the readout follows: the **KEY** label turns amber when the pitch or a key shift moved the
key away from the track's. A dot after it means the key was chosen by hand. See
[Tempo, sync and key](/docs/decks/sync/).

With four decks, the panels are on one line and the readouts lose their labels.

Click the **BPM** or **KEY** readout to open **Beat grid, key and key shift**: correct the
tempo and the grid, choose the key by hand, or transpose the track. The grid and key corrections
are explained in [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/), the key shift in
[Tempo, sync and key](/docs/decks/sync/#key-shift-and-harmonic-mixing).

## The deck menu

The **⋯** button at the top right of the panel opens what a deck does now and then. Each row
acts at once.

| Row | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Motor**: **BRAKE**, **SPINBACK** | Stops the deck as a turntable switched off, or spins the record back while held. See [Brake and spinback](/docs/decks/performance/#brake-and-spinback). |
| **Jump to**: **START**, **END** | **START** goes to the start of the track, playing or not as it was. **END** goes to the very end: a playing deck stops there, or starts again with **Repeat the track**. |
| **Reverse** | The track plays backwards. See [Reverse](/docs/decks/performance/#reverse). |
| **Mute the channel** | The deck is silent in the master but still heard in the headphones. |
| **Repeat the track** | At the end, the track goes on from its start without a gap. |
| **Tempo fader range** | **±8**, **±16** or **±50**: the travel of this deck's tempo fader. See [Tempo range](/docs/decks/sync/#tempo-range). |
| **Fader start** | With the software mixer only: raising the channel fader plays the deck, closing it stops it on its cue point. See [Fader start](/docs/decks/performance/#fader-start). |

While the track plays backwards or the channel is muted, a **REV** or **MUTE** key lights beside
**⋯**, so you cannot forget it: press it to turn it off.

**BRAKE** and **SPINBACK** are greyed out while a record drives the deck. **START** and **END**
are greyed out in absolute mode, where the record decides where the track is.

**Reverse**, **Mute the channel** and **Repeat the track** last until you quit. **KEY LOCK**, the
tempo fader range and **Fader start** are kept for the next start.

## Instant doubles

An instant double puts the track of the other deck of a pair on this one, at the same place:
same position, playing or not, the same loop. Decks pair as A with B, and C with D. During a roll
or a censor, the double takes the place the track will come back to.

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> | Deck A takes deck B's track |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> | Deck B takes deck A's track |

On a Mac, use <kbd>⌘</kbd> instead of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>. **Instant double on deck A** (and on the
other decks) is also in the command palette and can be mapped on a controller. As with any load,
Prisme asks first if the deck you double onto is playing.

## Eject a track

To take a track off a deck, run **Eject the track** from the command palette
(<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd>, then type "eject"), or map it on a controller. A playing deck asks
first. Loading another track also replaces the current one, so you rarely need to eject.

## Other actions for keys and controllers

These have no button on the deck. You can map them on a controller (see
[Actions](/docs/controllers/actions/)), and run the buttons among them from the command palette:

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **To the cue and play**, **To the cue, stopped**, **To the cue** | Go to the cue point, then play, stop, or keep playing or not |
| **The cue here** | Sets the cue point where the deck is, without stopping it |
| **To the start and play**, **To the start, stopped**, **To the start**, **To the end** | As **START** and **END** in the menu, with a choice of what follows |
| **Run through the track while held**, **Run back through the track while held** | Searches forwards or back at eight times the speed |
| **Place in the track (a touch strip)** | Puts the deck anywhere in the track, as the overview does |
