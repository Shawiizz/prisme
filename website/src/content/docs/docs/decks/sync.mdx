Beatmatching in Prisme works as on turntables: a tempo fader per deck and a way to nudge, with
the phase strip to show you where the beats are. **SYNC** can do it for you when you want it to,
and **KEY LOCK** and the key shift keep the mix in tune. All of it relies on the track's beat
grid: check it in [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/) if a track drifts for no reason.

## The tempo fader

The **PITCH** fader stands at the right end of each deck, the same way up as on a turntable:
**faster is down**. Its readout, beside **SYNC** on the rail, shows how far the tempo is from the
track's own, `+1.20 %`.

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Change the tempo | Drag the fader, or scroll over it |
| Change it by 0.1 % | Press **−** above the fader or **+** under it; hold to keep going |
| Go back to the track's tempo | Double-click the fader, or the pitch readout on the rail |

The deck's **BPM** readout shows the tempo you hear, and under it the track's own tempo with the
pitch applied (see [the readouts](/docs/decks/playing/#the-readouts)).

When **SYNC** is locked, or a record drives the deck, the fader no longer sets the tempo: it is
greyed out and shows the tempo the deck plays at.

### Tempo range

The range is how far the fader goes either way: **±8 %** (the default, as most turntables),
**±16 %** or **±50 %**. A smaller range gives finer control over the same travel.

- For every deck: **Settings** › **DJ** › **Tempo fader range**. Choosing it here puts every deck
  back on this range.
- For one deck: **Tempo fader range** in the deck menu **⋯**. That deck keeps its own range until
  you choose the general one again.

Both are kept for the next start. On a controller, **Tempo fader range: next** and
**Tempo fader range: previous** cycle a deck's range, and **Range of every tempo fader: next**
cycles them all.

### Nudging

To push a deck ahead or hold it back for a moment without changing its tempo:

| How | Deck A | Deck B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed up while held (+3 %) | <kbd>X</kbd> | <kbd>M</kbd> |
| Slow down while held (−3 %) | <kbd>Z</kbd> | <kbd>N</kbd> |

You can also slide the scrolling waveform as the edge of a jog wheel (see
[Move around the track](/docs/decks/playing/#move-around-the-track)), or use a controller's jog
wheel. Controllers also have **Speed up a little while held** and **Slow down a little while
held** (1 %), and **Tempo up 0.1 %** / **Tempo down 0.1 %**, which change the tempo and keep it.

The **«** and **»** keys beside **SYNC** push harder, at the pace of Serato's **−** and **+**: held,
the deck leaves its tempo by 2 % at once, then more and more. **«** slows the deck to a stop in
ten seconds, then plays it backwards, up to the tempo backwards; **»** speeds it up as much, to
twice the tempo in ten seconds and on. Let go, the deck is back at its tempo at once. Controllers and keys can have them too: **Faster and faster
while held** and **Slower and slower while held, then backwards**.

## The phase strip

Between the first two decks, the phase strip draws the beats of one deck above the line and the
other's below it, around their playheads; the first beat of each bar is brighter. When the two
decks are in phase, the beats meet across the line.

Beside **PHASE** it tells you, in words:

- **Matched**: the beats line up.
- **B ahead by 12 ms** or **B behind by 12 ms**: how far the second deck's beats are from the
  first's. Nudge it the other way.
- **Off by 1/16 in 40 s**: at the present tempos, the decks will drift a sixteenth apart (two
  kicks heard instead of one) in that time. Touch the pitch before then.
- **Off the beat: adjust the pitch**: they already have.

With four decks, the strip compares the first two decks that play, and names them (**PHASE A·C**).
A track at half or double the other's tempo (70 and 140 BPM) is compared beat for beat.

## SYNC

**SYNC** matches a deck to the leader's tempo and lines up its beats. It needs a beat grid
(without one, **SYNC** is greyed out with **No beat grid**).

It lines up the bars too, as Serato's beat sync and Pioneer's bar sync do: the deck jumps by up
to two beats so that its numbered bar lines fall on the leader's, and the phrases, drops and
breaks of both tracks come together. Where the bars of either track are to be checked (an
orange dot by its BPM, see [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/)), only the beats are lined up.

| Gesture | On a deck playing by itself |
| --- | --- |
| Tap **SYNC** | The deck locks to the leader: same tempo, beats held in phase, until you tap **SYNC** again. **SYNC** stays lit. |
| Hold **SYNC** (about half a second) | The deck matches the leader's tempo and beats once, and stays free. Its tempo fader moves to the new tempo. |

While a deck is locked, it keeps itself in phase with small speed corrections, and jumps back
into phase if it gets more than 0.08 of a beat off. Nudging a locked deck therefore
does not last, and sliding its waveform does not nudge it: to move its beats by hand, take
**SYNC** off. While it plays, a press on the overview of its whole track does not make it jump
either, so that a slip of the hand does not throw it out of the mix: pause it, or take **SYNC**
off, to jump there.

Taking **SYNC** off leaves the deck playing at the tempo it was locked to, as in rekordbox and
Serato: its tempo fader moves to that tempo. A controller's tempo fader takes over once you
bring it to that place (see [Keys, faders and knobs](/docs/controllers/#keys-faders-and-knobs)).

When the leader's track ends, or the leader is stopped, a locked deck that plays on has nothing
left to follow. After two seconds it takes the lead: its **SYNC** goes off the same way, it
keeps its tempo, and its tempo fader is yours again. Load the next track on the other deck and
press its **SYNC** to follow on. A leader scratched or held a moment keeps the lead.

A track at about half or double the leader's tempo locks to it beat for beat (70 BPM follows
140). A track whose grid changes tempo (pinned by hand, or imported from Serato or rekordbox)
follows from beat to beat: its speed changes with its grid, so that its beats stay on the
leader's. Loading a new track on a deck turns its **SYNC** off.

### The leader

The leader is the deck the others follow. Prisme picks it: the deck you chose if it can lead,
else the deck already leading if it still can, else the first deck that can. A deck can lead
when it plays forwards on a beat grid and is not locked itself; a deck that stops no longer
leads, so a follower is never left chasing beats that stand still. A locked deck without a
leader keeps the last tempo it had.

The **LEADER** tag appears on the rail of the leading deck. To choose the leader yourself, run
**Lead the other decks** for that deck from the command palette or a controller; run it on the
leading deck to let Prisme choose again. **Match the tempo and beats once** is the same as holding **SYNC**.

### SYNC with a record

A record in absolute mode decides where the track is, so **SYNC** is greyed out with **Sync needs
relative mode or internal play**. In relative mode, **SYNC** follows
**Settings** › **DJ** › **Sync on a record in relative mode**:

| Choice | What SYNC does |
| --- | --- |
| **One shot: matches once, the pitch fader keeps working** (default) | Matches the tempo and beats once. The turntable's pitch fader keeps working from there, and pressing **SYNC** again matches again after a drift. |
| **Locked: follows the leader, the pitch fader is ignored** | Locks the deck to the leader: the record's movements are heard around the leader's tempo. Taking **SYNC** off gives the tempo back to the turntable's pitch fader. |

On a record not locked by **SYNC**, the pitch readout shows an arrow to the pitch to set on your
turntable to match the other deck (`→ +1.40 %`), and the fader has a mark at that place. See
[Vinyl control modes](/docs/dvs/modes/).

## Quantize

**Quantize** puts what you do on the beat. It is on by default; turn it off in
**Settings** › **DJ** › **Quantize**, or map **Quantize** on a controller. With it on:

- a cue point, a hot cue, a loop in or a loop out lands on the nearest beat;
- a beat loop starts on the nearest beat (a loop shorter than a beat on the nearest step of its
  own length);
- a hot cue pressed while the deck plays starts on the next beat (at once, pressed right on a
  beat): pressed again and again, it starts again on each beat;
- a slice comes in where the beat is;
- a sampler sound waits for the next beat of its deck, when that deck plays;
- a stem muted or brought back changes on the next bar.

Without a beat grid, nothing is snapped.

## Key lock

**KEY LOCK**, on the rail under the pitch readout, keeps the key when the tempo changes: a track
sped up by 6 % stays in its key instead of rising by a semitone. It is set per deck and kept for
the next start. Controllers have **Key lock**.

While the motor moves the record (a brake, a spinback, a soft start) or your hand does, the key
lock lets go, so it sounds like a record.

## Key shift and harmonic mixing

The **KEY** readout shows the key you hear as a Camelot code: a number from 1 to 12 around the
wheel, **A** for minor keys and **B** for major ones. Two keys mix well when they are the same,
one step apart around the wheel with the same letter (`8A` and `9A`), or the same number with the
other letter (`8A` and `8B`).

The key shift transposes the track by semitones, up to 12 either way, without changing its
tempo. Click the **BPM** or **KEY** readout to open the editor; under **Key shift**:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **♭** / **♯** | Down or up a semitone |
| **Mix with the other deck** | The smallest shift (from 5 semitones down to 6 up) after which this deck mixes harmonically with the other one |
| **Reset** | Back to the track's key |

The other deck is the other deck playing, or else the one beside it (A with B, C with D).
**Mix with the other deck** is greyed out when either key is unknown.

The key shift lasts until you load another track on the deck. The **KEY** readout shows the
shifted key, its label in amber.

Controllers have **Key up a semitone**, **Key down a semitone**, **Key back to the track**,
**Key matched to the other deck**, and a fader for the key from 12 semitones down to 12 up.

<Aside type="tip">
Without **KEY LOCK**, the pitch moves the key too: about 6 % of tempo is a semitone. The **KEY**
readout follows the pitch, so you can always read the key you hear.
</Aside>

<Steps>

1. Load the next track on the deck that is not playing.
2. Compare the two **KEY** readouts. If they do not mix, open the editor (click **KEY**) and press
   **Mix with the other deck**.
3. Tap **SYNC** to lock the tempo and the beats to the playing deck, or match it by ear with the
   tempo fader, watching the phase strip.
4. Start the deck on the beat and bring it in.

</Steps>
