Prisme is built so that the music does not stop: the audio restarts by itself after a device
loss or an engine failure, and the decks come back after a crash. When something still goes
wrong, the title bar usually says so first. This page goes through what it can say, and what
to do. For problems with timecode records, see
[Timecode troubleshooting](/docs/dvs/troubleshooting/).

## The title bar

At the left of the title bar, a row of chips tells how the audio is doing:

| Chip | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| The driver and the device, such as **ASIO · UMC404HD** | The audio runs on that device. |
| **Buffer** with a length in ms | The length of an audio period, as the driver calls the engine. |
| **Dropouts** with a number | Periods processed too late since the audio started: each one is an audible click. Hover it for the slowest period. |
| **simulated turntable**, or **4 input ch** | Where the turntables are read from. |
| **audio device lost, reconnecting…** | The device went away; Prisme is bringing the audio back. |
| **audio stopped** | No audio is running. |
| **audio engine error** | The engine failed and could not be restarted. |
| **library not saved** | The library could not be opened: cues, corrections and crates changed now are lost at exit. |
| **External mixer needs 4 outputs** | Each deck needs its own pair of outputs, and the device does not have them. |

![The title bar, with the driver and device, the buffer length and the Dropouts counter at the left](../../../../assets/screens/decks.png)

## Audio dropouts

Clicks, crackles or short gaps in the sound, and the **Dropouts** counter climbing. Each
dropout is a period the computer did not finish in time.

- **Raise the buffer.** In **Settings**, **Audio**, **Buffer**, take the next size up, press
  **Apply**, and play for a while. Hover the **Dropouts** chip: it shows the slowest period;
  if it comes close to the length of a period, the buffer is too small for this computer.
- **Use the fastest driver.** On Windows, ASIO usually holds a smaller buffer than WASAPI.
  On Linux, JACK (PipeWire included) rather than ALSA through the sound server.
- **Leave the processor to the sound.** Keep **Pause the analysis of the library while a
  deck plays** on (**Settings**, **Library**). Separating stems on the processor alone, with
  no graphics chip to do it, takes a lot of it: separate before the set rather than during
  it.
- **Plug in the charger.** On battery, a laptop may slow its processor down. Choose the
  high-performance power mode of your system.
- **Close what you do not need.** Browsers, video calls and other audio software compete for
  the processor, and some for the sound card.
- **Plug the interface into the computer directly**, rather than through a hub.

<Aside type="tip">
The counter starts again from zero each time the audio restarts, so after a change you can
see at once whether it helped.
</Aside>

## The audio device is lost

When the interface is unplugged, switched off or its driver resets, the title bar shows
**audio device lost, reconnecting…**. Prisme tries again at once and several times within
the next second, then every two seconds. As soon as the device is back, the audio restarts
with the decks where they were, playing if they were.

If the interface you chose is missing when Prisme starts, it plays on the system's default
device meanwhile, keeps your choice in the settings, and moves to your interface as soon as
it appears.

## The audio engine error

**audio engine error** means the audio engine failed. Prisme restarts it by itself, up to
three times a minute; beyond that the failure would only come back, so it stays silent. Open
**Settings**, **Audio**, change nothing and apply again, or choose another buffer or driver
and press **Apply**. Then save a diagnostic report (see below) and send it: this should not
happen.

## No sound, or audio stopped

- **audio stopped**: the audio could not start. Open **Settings**, **Audio**, choose the
  **Output** (or another **Driver type**) and press **Apply**. If it fails, the reason shows
  at the bottom of the page.
- The audio runs but nothing is heard: check **Mixing** in **Settings**, **Mixing**. With
  **External mixer**, deck A plays on outputs 1-2 and deck B on 3-4, into a hardware mixer;
  with nothing plugged into those outputs you hear nothing. With **Software mixer**, the
  master is on outputs 1-2.
- On Android, if Prisme was not allowed to listen to the inputs, the settings say **allow
  Prisme to listen to the inputs of the sound card, then apply again**.

## After a crash

While Prisme runs, it notes every few seconds which track each deck holds and where. If it
did not close normally, the next start loads those tracks again on their decks, at the
position they had, stopped. Your settings, cues, crates and library are saved as you go and
are not affected.

## The library is not saved

**library not saved** in the title bar means the library database could not be opened, so
Prisme works from memory: the cues, grid corrections and crates you change now are lost when
you quit. Quit and start Prisme again; if it persists, make sure the data folder (see
[Where Prisme keeps its files](#where-prisme-keeps-its-files)) is writable and the disk not
full, and send a diagnostic report.

## Analysis is slow

The library shows **Analysing** with a count while it works out tempo, beat grid and key in
the background. With **Pause the analysis of the library while a deck plays** on, it reads
**Analysis paused** while a deck plays and **Goes on once the decks stop**. That is on
purpose, to keep the processor for the sound. A track you load is analysed as it loads,
whatever the queue.

To let the analysis run, stop the decks, or turn that setting off when you are not playing.
See [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/).

## Stem separation is slow

The library shows **Separating** with a count while stems are separated; hover it to see
what does the work, a graphics chip or the processor.

- **On a graphics chip**, a track takes seconds. Check that **Hardware acceleration** is on
  in **Settings**, **Library**. If it reads **No graphics chip worth it here: the processor
  separates.**, the computer has none Prisme can use.
- **On the processor alone**, a track takes minutes. Switch **Separation** to **Fast**: one
  small model, several times faster. Its stems are not as clean.
- **Drums and bass** take two to three times as long as the vocals alone.
- Separate before the set: **Separate the whole library**, or put the tracks in Prepare
  with **Separate the tracks as they are loaded or prepared** on.

See [Stems](/docs/sound/stems/).

## A controller is not seen

<Aside type="note">
A controller is not used until you switch it on, once, in **Settings**, **MIDI
controllers**. After that it is opened at each start, and again by itself if it is
unplugged and plugged back in.
</Aside>

- **It is not in the list**: Prisme reads **No MIDI controller plugged in.** Plug it in,
  switch it on if it has a power switch, and press **Refresh**. Try another USB port or cable,
  and plug it into the computer directly rather than through a hub.
- **It is listed but does nothing**: turn on its switch in the list.
- **Another program holds it**: on Windows, a MIDI port can be opened by one program at a
  time. Quit other DJ software and MIDI tools, then switch the controller off and on again in
  the list.
- **It is not mapped**: a model Prisme knows reads **Mapped as a…** under its name. Another
  needs its controls learnt: see [MIDI learn](/docs/controllers/learn/). Prisme fetches new
  profiles once a day; **Look for updates** fetches them now.
- **Some controls do the wrong thing**: profiles marked **Converted from the mapping of Mixxx,
  not yet tried on this controller** may need a correction. Learn the control again, and see
  [Profiles](/docs/controllers/profiles/) to share the fix.

## Logs and the diagnostic report

Prisme writes a log, one file per day, keeping the last seven days. To send it with what
surrounds it, use the diagnostic report:

1. Open **Settings**, **About**.
2. Press **Save a diagnostic report…** and choose where to save it. The button then reads
   **Report saved**.

The report is a plain text file holding the version of Prisme and of your system, the audio
settings you saved and those running, the audio devices and their health (dropouts,
the slowest period, device losses), the controllers in use, and the last 400 lines of the
log. Read it before you send it: the log names the files you played.

The log files themselves are in:

| System | Log folder |
| --- | --- |
| Windows | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\com.shawiizz.prisme\logs` |
| macOS | `~/Library/Logs/com.shawiizz.prisme` |
| Linux | `~/.local/share/com.shawiizz.prisme/logs` |

## Where Prisme keeps its files

Your settings (`preferences.json`), your controllers and what you mapped
(`controllers.json`), the library (`library.sqlite`) and your own controller profiles (the
`controllers` folder) are in the data folder:

| System | Data folder |
| --- | --- |
| Windows | `%APPDATA%\com.shawiizz.prisme` |
| macOS | `~/Library/Application Support/com.shawiizz.prisme` |
| Linux | `~/.local/share/com.shawiizz.prisme` |

Separated stems are not there: they sit next to each track, in a `.prisme` folder.

## Report a problem

Open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/Shawiizz/prisme/issues) with what you did,
what you expected, what happened, and the diagnostic report attached.
