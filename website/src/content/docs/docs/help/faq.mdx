Short answers to the questions people ask first. Each links to the page that goes further.

## What does Prisme cost?

Nothing. Prisme is free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3: you can
use it, share it and change it, and its source is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/Shawiizz/prisme). There is no account, no subscription and no
feature held back.

One part has a licence of its own: the small, fast separation model (chosen with
**Separation**: **Fast**) is under CC BY-NC 4.0, which forbids commercial use. The large
models of **Quality** are under MIT and Apache 2.0.

## Which systems does it run on?

- Windows 10 and 11.
- macOS 13.3 or later. The macOS build is not notarized by Apple: the first time, open it
  from System Settings, Privacy & Security.
- Linux, as a `.deb` package or an AppImage. It plays through ALSA, or through JACK (PipeWire
  included) for the lowest latency.
- Android, on a phone or a tablet.

There is no iOS version. See [Install Prisme](/docs/start/install/).

## Do I need turntables?

No. Every deck plays by itself too, driven from the screen, the keyboard, the
[command palette](/docs/interface/keyboard/) or a [MIDI controller](/docs/controllers/).
Turntables are what Prisme was built around, not a requirement.

## Which timecode records work?

Timecode records and CDs from Serato today: the Control Vinyl CV02, side A or side B, and
the Control CD. More formats are to come; the timecodes of Traktor, rekordbox and Mixxx are
not read yet. Each turntable has its own record, chosen in its panel of **Settings**,
**Turntables**: side A on one and side B or the CD on the other works. See
[Vinyl control](/docs/dvs/).

## Which sound card do I need for vinyl control?

One with a stereo input per turntable that can take a turntable's phono signal (phono inputs,
or a phono/line switch), or a phono preamplifier in front of line inputs. Four input
channels for two turntables. ASIO on Windows gives the lowest latency. See
[Setting up timecode vinyl](/docs/dvs/setup/).

## Can I use my hardware mixer?

Yes, that is the default on a computer: each deck comes out on its own pair of outputs
(deck A on 1-2, deck B on 3-4) into a channel of your mixer. Or use the software mixer, with
the master on outputs 1-2 and the headphones on 3-4. See [Mixer](/docs/mixer/mixer/).

## Two turntables and four decks?

Yes. On an interface for two turntables, deck C shares the turntable of deck A and deck D
that of deck B; the deck you put on REL or ABS takes it. With eight inputs, each deck has its
own. See [Layouts](/docs/interface/layouts/).

## Which files does it play?

MP3, AAC, ALAC, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis, WAV and AIFF, from your own folders. Streaming services
are not supported.

## Can I bring my Serato or rekordbox library?

Yes: crates, cues and beat grids come across with **Import**, in the library. See
[Import](/docs/library/import/).

## Can I take my sets to rekordbox and the CDJs?

Yes: **Export**, in the library, writes your library or chosen lists as rekordbox's XML, with
the beat grids, keys, cues, loops and playlists; rekordbox reads it and puts the tracks on a USB
stick for the CDJs. Any list also exports as an M3U playlist for other software. See
[Exporting to rekordbox and playlists](/docs/library/export/).

## Does Prisme change my music files?

On a computer it writes the tempo and key it finds into the tags of your files, so that other
DJ software reads them; values another program wrote are kept. Turn it off in **Settings**,
**Library**, **Write the tempo and key into the tags of the files**. On a phone this is off
by default.

Separated stems are stored next to each track, in a `.prisme` folder. Cues, grids and crates
are kept in Prisme's own library.

## Does it need the internet?

No, it plays offline. It goes online for three things only, all to GitHub: to look for a new
version at each start, to fetch new controller profiles once a day, and to download a stem
separation model when you ask for it. It sends nothing about you or your music: there is no
account, no tracking and no usage statistics.

## Which controllers work?

MIDI controllers. 57 models are mapped out of the box, from Allen & Heath, Behringer, Denon,
DJ TechTools, Hercules, Korg, Native Instruments, Numark, Pioneer, Reloop, Roland and
Stanton; any other can be mapped with MIDI learn. Controllers that speak HID rather than MIDI
are not supported. See [Supported controllers](/docs/controllers/supported/).

## Does it sync with other software or gear?

It sends MIDI clock, 24 ticks per beat of the sync leader, from **Settings**, **MIDI
controllers**, **MIDI clock out**. Ableton Link is not supported.

## Can I record my mix?

Yes, with its tracklist, from **Rec** in the title bar. See [Recording](/docs/mixer/recording/).

## Which languages?

English and French. Prisme follows the language of your system and can be switched in
**Settings**, **Interface**, **Language**.

## What is not there?

To save you looking: no timecode other than Serato's yet, no HID controllers, no Ableton Link,
no streaming services, no video, no iOS version.

## Something does not work. Where do I go?

See [Troubleshooting](/docs/help/troubleshooting/) and
[Timecode troubleshooting](/docs/dvs/troubleshooting/). To report a problem, open an issue
on [GitHub](https://github.com/Shawiizz/prisme/issues) with a diagnostic report from
**Settings**, **About**.
