Vinyl control (DVS, for digital vinyl system) lets a turntable drive a deck. Instead of
music, the record on the platter carries a timecode: a steady tone with a sequence of bits
pressed into it. Prisme listens to that signal through your sound card and works out, many
times a second, how fast the record turns, in which direction, and where the needle sits on
it. The deck then plays your track the same way: nudge the platter and the track nudges,
scratch and it scratches, drop the needle further in and the track jumps there.

Prisme was built for this first. Its timecode decoder is written from scratch and runs inside
the audio engine, so the record and the sound stay together.

## What Prisme reads

Prisme reads timecode records and CDs. Today it knows Serato's; the timecodes of other
makers are to come.

| Control medium | In Prisme |
| --- | --- |
| Serato Control Vinyl CV02, side A | **Serato** › **Control vinyl CV02**, side **A** |
| Serato Control Vinyl CV02, side B | **Serato** › **Control vinyl CV02**, side **B** |
| Serato Control CD, on a CD player | **Serato** › **Control CD** |

The timecodes of Traktor, rekordbox and Mixxx are not read yet. Each turntable has its own
record, chosen in its panel of **Settings**, **Turntables**: one deck can play side A while
the other plays side B, or a CD player with the control CD sit beside a turntable. See
[The record of each turntable](/docs/dvs/setup/#the-record-of-each-turntable).

The usable part of each medium, at 33 rpm, before the needle reaches the end of the
timecode:

| Medium | Usable length at 33 rpm |
| --- | --- |
| CV02 side A | about 10 minutes |
| CV02 side B | about 15 minutes |
| Control CD | about 15 minutes |

A deck can also treat its record as a 45 rpm one: the track then spreads over more of the
groove, so a side holds less of it (about 7 minutes on side A). See
[Setting up timecode vinyl](/docs/dvs/setup/#record-speed-33-or-45).

## What it does

- **Absolute mode** (ABS): the needle position is the track position. Needle drops, cues
  you find by eye on the record, everything as with a real record.
- **Relative mode** (REL): the record drives the speed only. Hot cues, loops, beat jumps
  and sync work on top of it.
- **INT**: the deck plays by itself, as without turntables.
- **THRU**: the deck plays what comes into its inputs, a real record or a CD player.
- Backspins, scratches and needle bounces are followed; when the needle reaches the end of
  the timecode while the track plays, the deck plays on by itself instead of stopping.

The modes are explained in [Absolute and relative modes](/docs/dvs/modes/).

## What you need

**Turntables or CD players.** Any turntable with a cartridge in good shape, or a CD player
for the control CD.

**Timecode records or CDs.** Serato Control Vinyl (CV02, side A or B), or the Serato
Control CD, whichever on each turntable.

**A sound card with a stereo input for each turntable.** Each turntable takes a pair of
input channels: inputs 1-2 for deck A, 3-4 for deck B. A card with four input channels
therefore serves two turntables, and one with eight serves four.

A turntable's output is a weak phono-level signal. The input has to bring it up: DJ
interfaces made for DVS have phono inputs, or a switch per input to set to phono. A card
with line inputs only needs a phono preamplifier between turntable and card. A CD player
playing the control CD gives a line-level signal and goes into a line input. More on this
in [Setting up timecode vinyl](/docs/dvs/setup/#phono-or-line).

**Outputs for the mix.** Either each deck on its own output pair, into a hardware mixer
(outputs 1-2 for deck A, 3-4 for deck B), or Prisme's software mixer with the master on
outputs 1-2 and your headphones on 3-4. See [Mixer](/docs/mixer/mixer/).

<Aside type="tip" title="No turntables yet?">
Turn on **Simulated turntable** in **Settings**, **Turntables**: Prisme plays a
turntable of its own into deck A, which plays for four seconds and then scratches back and
forth for two, over and over. It lets you see how the decks follow a record before you plug
anything in.
</Aside>

## Two turntables, four decks

With four decks on an interface for two turntables, deck C shares the turntable, its record
and the outputs of deck A, and deck D those of deck B. The deck you put on REL or ABS takes the
turntable; the other one leaves the record and goes back to INT. With eight inputs, each of
the four decks has a turntable of its own. See
[Choose the inputs](/docs/dvs/setup/#choose-the-inputs).

## On a phone or a tablet

On Android, vinyl control works through a USB sound card with inputs plugged into the
phone. Android asks whether Prisme may listen to the inputs the first time they are needed;
if you refuse, Prisme cannot read the timecode until you allow it and apply the settings
again. See [Prisme on a phone](/docs/interface/phone/).
