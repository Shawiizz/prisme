A deck handed to its turntable follows the record in one of two modes. In absolute mode
(**ABS**) the record is the track: where the needle sits is where the track plays. In
relative mode (**REL**) the record only gives the speed, and the deck keeps its own idea of
where it is, so cues, loops and sync can move it. Two more buttons sit beside them on the
deck card: **INT**, the deck playing by itself, and **THRU**, the deck playing its inputs.

You switch between them at any time on the deck card, even mid-track. See
[Hand a deck to its record](/docs/dvs/setup/#hand-a-deck-to-its-record).

## Which mode for what

| You want to | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| Play as with real records: needle drops, places found by eye on the record | ABS |
| Use hot cues, loops, beat jumps, the slicer, slip | REL |
| Use SYNC on a deck driven by a record | REL |
| Scratch hard with a needle that may skip | REL |
| Play a real record or a CD player through a deck | THRU |

## Absolute mode (ABS)

The position of the needle on the record is the position in the track. Lift the needle and
drop it a minute further in: the track jumps a minute ahead. Spin the record back to the
start: the track is back at its start.

Prisme follows the record continuously. Small differences between the track and the record
(a fraction of a second) are closed smoothly by speeding the track up or slowing it down for
a moment, so nothing clicks; a larger one, a tenth of a second or more, is a needle drop and
the track jumps.

Because the record decides where the track is, nothing else can move it:

- **SYNC** is unavailable; its button says **Sync needs relative mode or internal play**.
- The pads are greyed out in every mode but FX and SMPL: no hot cues, loops, rolls, jumps,
  slices or cuts. So are the loop keys and **CENSOR**.
- The reverse does not play the track backwards.
- A controller's jog wheel does nothing.

To match tempos by ear and by eye, next to the deck's pitch readout an arrow shows the pitch
to set on the turntable to match the other deck, such as **→ +1.20 %**. The phase strip
between the waveforms tells when the beats drift apart: **Off by 1/16 in 12 s**, then
**Off the beat: adjust the pitch**. See [Sync and beatmatching](/docs/decks/sync/).

### Needle drops

Put the needle down anywhere and the deck follows once Prisme has read where it is, after a
short stretch of clean signal. Until then the deck takes the record's speed only. A position
read after a drop is checked over the same stretch before the track jumps there, so a
crackle or a scratch on the record does not send the track elsewhere.

### The lead-in

The track does not start at the very edge of the record: on the Serato records it starts 28
seconds in, on the control CD at once. The grooves before are where you put the needle down;
the deck plays silence there, its track waiting at the start, and the music begins when the
needle reaches that point. Set another start in the **Lead-in (s)** field of the turntable's
panel, see [Lead-in](/docs/dvs/setup/#lead-in).

## Relative mode (REL)

The record gives the speed and the direction only. Where the track is stays the deck's: a
needle drop, or the needle lifted and put back elsewhere, does not move it, the track just
goes on from where it was once the record turns again.

Everything that moves the track works on top of the record: hot cues, loops, loop rolls,
beat jumps, the slicer, slip, censor and reverse. Press a hot cue and the track
jumps there while the record keeps turning; it carries on from the cue at the speed of the
platter.

### SYNC on a record

A turntable has its own pitch fader, so SYNC on a record can work two ways. Choose in
**Settings**, **DJ**, **Sync on a record in relative mode**:

| Choice | SYNC |
| --- | --- |
| **One shot: matches once, the pitch fader keeps working** (default) | Pressing SYNC snaps the beats onto the leader's and matches its tempo once. From then on your turntable's pitch fader changes the tempo from there; the beats are not corrected afterwards. |
| **Locked: follows the leader, the pitch fader is ignored** | Tap SYNC to lock the deck to the leader's tempo; hold it to match once instead. Locked, the turntable's pitch fader no longer changes the tempo, but nudges and scratches on the platter still do. |

In both, the phase stays in your hands once matched: Prisme never pulls the beats back into
line behind your back. Loading another track, or changing the record speed, forgets the
match. More in [Sync and beatmatching](/docs/decks/sync/).

## INT

The deck plays by itself with its own PLAY button, CUE and tempo fader, as without
turntables. Choosing REL or ABS again hands it back to the record.

## THRU

The deck plays what comes into its inputs, as it comes: a real record, a CD player, any line
source. Its track waits where it was. The deck's effects still act on the sound, and with
the software mixer so do its channel strip and fader, so a real record can be mixed in with
the rest. Choose INT, REL or ABS to hear the track again; the vinyl settings are left as they
were.

## The end of the record

When the needle reaches the end of the timecode while the track plays forward (at least at
half speed), the deck does not stop with the music: it plays on by itself at the speed the
record last had. The deck card then shows **INT**, and its light says **The needle reached
the end of the record: the deck plays on by itself. Choose REL or ABS to hand it back to
the record.**

How the record takes it back depends on the mode:

- **REL**: as soon as the needle is back before the end and the platter turns, the record
  drives the deck again, by itself.
- **ABS**: taking it back would make the track jump to where the needle is, so it waits for
  you. Press **REL** or **ABS** on the deck card, or load another track.

The deck stops when its track ends. This only happens once the record has been read before
its end since the track was loaded: a track loaded with the needle already in the run-out
grooves does not set it off.

The end of the record comes after about 10 minutes on side A at 33 rpm, 15 on side B
(see [What Prisme reads](/docs/dvs/#what-prisme-reads)). Longer tracks either play on by
themselves past it, or go on in REL from a needle put back near the start.

## Scratching and backspins

Both modes follow the record forwards and backwards, slow or fast, so scratches and
backspins sound as they are played. A few things help:

- **Key lock** keeps the pitch while the tempo changes, between half and twice the speed;
  outside that range, during a scratch or a backspin, the track is pitched as a real record
  would be.
- Held still under a finger, the track barely sounds, and dragged slowly it is softer, as a
  needle in a groove. A scratch is at full level.
- Needle bounces are held: when the signal drops for a moment while the record turns, the
  speed is kept until it comes back, and the turntable's panel reads **unsteady**. Clicks of
  dust on the record are ignored.
- In ABS a needle that skips makes the track skip by the same amount. REL ignores skips.
- For cuts, set the crossfader curve to **SHARP** in **Settings**, **Mixing**, or use a
  hardware mixer's own curve.
