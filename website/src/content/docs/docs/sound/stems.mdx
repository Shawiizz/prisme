Prisme separates a track into its parts so a deck can play each one at its own level: the
**vocals**, the **drums**, the **bass**, and the **melody** (everything else). Mute the vocals for an
instrumental, keep only the drums to mix over, or bring the bass of the next track in on the bar.
Separation runs on your graphics card or your processor, in the background, and the stems are kept
next to each track for the next time.

![Two decks: deck A's track is separated into four stems, with its VOX, DRUM, BASS and MELO knobs and the drums muted; deck B's track is not separated and shows a SEPARATE key](../../../../assets/screens/decks.png)

## Install the models

Separation needs models, downloaded once from the Prisme releases. Two setups are offered, in
**Settings** > **Library** > **Separation**:

| Choice | Model | Size | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Quality** | **Vocals separation** | 456 MB | Vocals and instrumental |
| | **Drums and bass separation** (needs the vocals model) | 979 MB | Drums, bass and melody out of the instrumental |
| **Fast** | **Separation into stems** | 91 MB | Vocals, drums, bass and melody from one small model |

**Quality** gives the cleanest stems and is the one to use with a graphics card worth it. **Fast** is
several times faster, for a phone or a computer without one. With only the vocals model, tracks are
split in two (vocals and instrumental); add the drums and bass model to split them in four.

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** > **Library**.

2. Under **Separation**, choose **Quality** or **Fast**.

3. Press **Download (… MB)** on each model of that setup. The percentage shows the download; then the
   row reads **Installed**. A broken download resumes where it stopped.

</Steps>

If you have both setups installed, Prisme uses the one chosen under **Separation**; with only one, it
uses that one whatever the choice.

<Aside type="note">
The vocals model is Kimberley Jensen's Mel-Band RoFormer (MIT licence), the drums and bass model
SYH99999's four-stem Mel-Band RoFormer (Apache 2.0 licence). The fast model is HiDolen's
Mini-BS-RoFormer-V2.5, under the CC BY-NC 4.0 licence: not for commercial use.
</Aside>

## Separate a track

There are several ways, all of which put the track in the same background queue:

- **On the deck**: a deck whose track is not separated shows a **SEPARATE** key where the stem knobs
  go. Press it; its percentage shows how far it is.
- **As you load it**: with **Separate the tracks as they are loaded or prepared** on, a track loaded on
  a deck is separated at once, starting from where the deck plays, and a track added to the prepare
  list is separated in the background, ready before the set.
- **In the library**: the **STEMS** column has a **+** on each track that can be separated; right-click
  tracks for **Separate the stems**, or use **Separate the stems** in the bar of a selection.
- **The whole library**: **Settings** > **Library** > **Separate the whole library** > **Separate**.

The library's status bar shows **Separating 3 / 12** with a progress bar while the queue runs; hover
it to see whether the graphics card or the processor does the work, and press **Stop** to empty the
queue. A track loaded on a deck always goes first: a library track being separated gives way and
starts again afterwards.

In the **STEMS** column, a track shows its percentage while it is separated, **…** while it waits,
**✓** once its vocals are separated, and **4** once it is separated into four. A **+** on a track
with a **✓** adds the drums and bass.

### While the track plays

A track separated as it plays gets its stems from where the deck is, first there and then ahead, so
they come within seconds on a good graphics card. Until a part is separated where the deck plays, its
knob is greyed out and the track plays whole there. Tracks longer than 20 minutes (a recorded mix,
say) are not separated as they load; press **SEPARATE** or separate them from the library.

Stopped midway (**Stop** in the library's bar), a separation leaves the deck the stems it made, where
it made them: their knobs act there, the track plays whole elsewhere, and a **SEPARATE** key beside
the knobs separates it again.

## The stem knobs

Once a track is separated, its deck shows a knob per part on the rail, in place of the **SEPARATE**
key:

| Separated into | Knobs |
| --- | --- |
| Vocals | **VOX** (vocals), **INST** (instrumental: everything else) |
| Four stems | **VOX** (vocals), **DRUM** (drums), **BASS** (bass), **MELO** (melody, the other instruments) |

- **Turn** a knob to set that part's level, from silent to `100 %`, as it is in the track.
- **Click its name** to mute the part; click again to bring it back. A muted part keeps its level: its
  knob dims and its name lights.

The knobs come back to `100 %` with each track you load.

On a phone, the deck's **STEMS** key opens the same knobs. See [On a phone](/docs/interface/phone/).

### Cuts on the bar

With **Quantize** on (**Settings** > **DJ** > **Quantize**), muting or bringing back a part waits for
the next bar: press the name anywhere during a bar and the cut happens as the next one starts. The
name blinks while it waits. A bar is four beats of the deck's beat grid, so the grid must be right
(see [Analysis](/docs/library/analysis/)).

Turning a knob always acts at once. A deck that is stopped, or a track without a beat grid, cuts at
once too.

From a controller, each deck has a key per part, **Vocals**, **Instrumental**, **Drums**, **Bass**
and **Melody (the other instruments)**, lit while the part plays, and a fader for each level
(**Vocals level** and so on). See [all actions](/docs/controllers/actions/).

## The lanes

Under a separated track's overview, thin lanes show where each part plays along the whole track:
two lanes (vocals and instrumental) or four (vocals, drums, bass and melody). They show at a glance
where the vocals come in, where the drums drop out, where the breakdown is. Click or drag along them
to move in the track, as on the overview. The lanes appear once the whole track is separated.

## Effects on a stem

A deck's effect chain can play on one part alone: an echo on the vocals only, a gate on the drums.
See [Effects on a stem](/docs/sound/effects/#on-a-stem).

## Settings

All in **Settings** > **Library**:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Separation** | **Quality** (the large models) or **Fast** (one small model). |
| **Vocals separation**, **Drums and bass separation**, **Separation into stems** | Download each model. |
| **Hardware acceleration** | Lets the graphics chip separate, many times faster than the processor. Greyed out with **No graphics chip worth it here: the processor separates.** when there is none. |
| **Separate the tracks as they are loaded or prepared** | Separates each track loaded on a deck right away, and each track put in the prepare list in the background. On at first with a graphics card, off without one. |
| **Separate the whole library** | Queues every track of the library that can be separated. |

## Where the stems are kept

The stems are stored next to each track, in a `.prisme` folder, as FLAC files. Copy your music folder
to another computer and its separations come along: Prisme finds them and does not separate those
tracks again. On a phone, where nothing may be written beside the shared music, they go in Prisme's
own folder instead.

The models themselves go in Prisme's local data folder (on Windows,
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\com.shawiizz.prisme\models`), out of roaming profiles.

## Performance tips

- **A graphics card makes the difference.** On a recent one, a track takes a few seconds; on the
  processor alone, a few minutes. Prisme uses the graphics card through Vulkan, Direct3D 12 or
  Metal, with nothing to install, and its tensor cores where Vulkan reaches them. The models fit on a
  4 GB card. Integrated graphics on a PC are left out, being slower than the processor; Apple's and
  a phone's are used.
- **No graphics card?** Choose **Fast**, and separate your crates before the gig: add the tracks to
  the prepare list, or run **Separate the whole library** overnight. On the processor, separation uses
  all the cores but two, at a low priority, so the sound comes first.
- **Separate before the set.** Separated tracks load with their stems at once, with nothing running
  in the background while you play.
- If the graphics card cannot be opened, or fails twice, Prisme goes on with the processor until
  you restart it.

<Aside type="tip">
The separation keeps the models open for five minutes after the queue empties, so the next track you
load starts separating at once.
</Aside>
