On a phone or a tablet, Prisme runs the same engine and the same library as on a computer, laid out for the fingers. The decks follow what is plugged in: with nothing, you play them as a small table under your thumbs; with a controller or turntables, they turn into a display to read from a step away. Prisme is available on Android for now: see [Install Prisme](/docs/start/install/).

## The screen

**At the bottom**, three tabs: **Decks**, **Library** and **Settings**.

**At the top**, a bar with:

- the audio: the driver, the device and the length of a period, with a green light; or **audio stopped**, or **audio device lost, reconnecting…**;
- **Controls**, on the **Decks** tab, which switches between the two ways of showing the decks (see below);
- the search button, which opens the [command palette](/docs/interface/keyboard/#command-palette) over the whole screen;
- **Rec**, which records the master of the software mixer, or the first inputs of the sound card when a hardware mixer plays into it. See [Recording your mix](/docs/mixer/recording/).

The **back gesture** closes what was opened last: a panel, the palette, a page of the settings.

Several fingers work at once: you can press both **PLAY** keys together, or hold a pad while moving a fader.

## Decks: played with the thumbs

With no controller in use and no deck on a record, the **Decks** tab is a table, its **Controls** key lit:

- the waveforms of the two decks at the top, each with its title, its BPM, its key and its remaining time;
- with the software mixer, a channel per deck in the middle: EQ and filter, the fader with its level, and the headphones button. The crossfader runs under them, between **A** and **B**, with **MIC** when a microphone is set up and **⋯**, the mixer's sheet (master level and balance, booth, limiter, crossfader curve, headphones, microphone, aux line);
- under each thumb, the keys of its deck: **CUE**, **PLAY** and **SYNC**, **PLAY** in the corner. Deck B's are the mirror of deck A's.

### Upright

Held upright, each side has four keys over its deck's **CUE**, **PLAY** and **SYNC**. Each opens a panel floating over the table, with the deck's letter at the top and a key to close it:

| Key | Opens |
| --- | --- |
| **PADS** | The deck's pads, their modes and their keys, as on a computer. See [Hot cues](/docs/decks/cues/). |
| **PITCH** | The tempo fader across the screen, **−** and **+** to bend the tempo while held, **0** to bring it back to the track's, and **KEY LOCK**. On a record, the pitch to set on the turntable. |
| **STEMS** | A knob per part of the separated track, its name muting it; **Separate** if the track is not separated yet. See [Stems](/docs/sound/stems/). |
| **DECK** | The deck's settings (see [below](#a-decks-settings)). |

### Sideways

Held sideways, the pads and the keys of each deck go down its side of the screen, A on the left and B on the right, the table between them, **SYNC** over **CUE** and **PLAY**. **STEMS** stays a key on each side; touch a deck's title to open its settings.

### Four decks

With four decks, A and C share the left side, B and D the right. A key with their letters, on each side, chooses the deck that side plays.

## Decks: read from a step away

When a controller is in use, or a deck is driven by a record, your hands are elsewhere: the **Decks** tab becomes a display.

- Each deck is large: its letter, cover, title and artist, its key, a wide waveform, then the remaining time in large figures, who drives it (**REL**, **ABS** or **THRU**, coloured by the timecode signal), **REV** or **MUTE** when on, the BPM and the pitch.
- The phase strip sits between the two decks.
- Under them, the library peeks out: its search and the tracks, ready to load.

On a screen too low, a phone held sideways, each deck keeps its waveform and a single line over it.

Press **Controls** to switch to the table anyway, or back. Your choice is kept, at the next start too: Prisme no longer chooses by itself.

## A deck's settings

Touch the title of a deck, or **DECK**, and its settings rise from the bottom:

![The settings of deck A risen from the bottom on a phone held upright: Driven by Itself, Vinyl REL, Vinyl ABS or Its input, the pitch fader, KEY LOCK and Grid and key, then Motor, Jump to, Reverse, Mute the channel, Repeat the track, the tempo fader range and Fader start](../../../../assets/screens/docs/phone-deck-sheet.png)

- **Deck A · driven by**: **Itself**, **Vinyl REL**, **Vinyl ABS** or **Its input**, when turntables are set up, with what the timecode signal says. See [Vinyl control modes](/docs/dvs/modes/).
- **Pitch**, the tempo fader across the screen.
- **Mixer**, outside the table with the software mixer: wide faders for the EQ, the filter and the volume.
- **Track**: **KEY LOCK**, **Grid and key** to correct the beat grid and the key, and, outside the table with the software mixer, the headphones button.
- The deck's options: the motor, the jumps to the start and the end, reverse, mute, repeat, the tempo range.

Touch the bar at its top, touch outside it, or use the back gesture to close it.

## The waveform under a finger

The waveform of each deck works as a jog wheel, for any finger:

| Gesture | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Slide at once along the waveform | Nudges the track, as the edge of a jog wheel: pushed ahead or held back while playing, moved while stopped |
| Press, hold still a moment, then slide | Your hand on the record: the deck follows your finger, as a record scratched, and plays on when you lift it |
| Press and slide along the overview | Jumps there and moves through the track |

A tap on the large waveform never makes the track jump, since a finger may land there by mistake. To jump, use the overview.

## Library

The **Library** tab lists your tracks in large rows: the cover, the title and the artist, the BPM, and the key, filled in when it fits the deck you compare with. A folder's sub-folders come first, and **Parent folder** goes up.

Above the rows: the search, the sort order, then keys to search the list you are in or the whole **Library**, to open **Prepare**, and **Browse…**, which rises with the same lists as the side of the library on a computer (folders, **Prepare**, **Duplicates**, crates, **History**) and **Import from Serato or rekordbox**.

| Gesture | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Slide a track to the right | Loads it on deck A |
| Slide a track to the left | Loads it on deck B |
| Touch a track | Opens what you can do with it: load it on a deck, add it to the prepare list or a crate, colour, genre, stems |
| Headphones key of a row | Previews the track in the headphones; touch it again to stop |

A track slid only part of the way comes back without loading. The line at the top of the list reminds you of the directions: **Slide a track right for A, left for B**.

Prisme reads the shared **Music** folder of the phone. Copy your tracks there from a computer, or with the phone's file manager. See [Adding your music](/docs/library/adding-music/).

## Settings

The **Settings** tab lists the pages of the settings, each with what it holds, under a search over every setting. Touch a page to open it; the back arrow or the back gesture returns to the list. The pages are those of a computer, but for **Keyboard shortcuts**. See [Settings](/docs/settings/).

## Sound

On a phone, Prisme mixes by itself: the software mixer is the default, the master on outputs 1-2.

The phone alone plays through its speaker or its headphones socket: there is no second pair of outputs for the headphones, so you cannot cue a track before the crowd hears it. For that, plug in a USB sound card with four outputs: the master goes out on 1-2, the headphones on 3-4. Set them in **Settings** > **Mixing** > **Headphones**. See [Headphones and preview](/docs/mixer/headphones/).

### USB sound cards

A USB sound card plugged into the phone takes over the outputs: with the **AAudio** driver, Android sends the sound to it.

Some cards reach Android with only part of their channels, or with inputs that stay silent. Then let Prisme drive the card itself:

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** > **Audio** and choose **USB** in **Driver type**.

2. In **USB sound card**, choose your card, or **The first plugged in**.

3. Press **Apply**. Android asks whether Prisme may use the card: allow it.

4. For turntables, open **Settings** > **Turntables** and turn on **Turntables plugged into the inputs of this driver**. Android asks whether Prisme may record audio: allow it, since the timecode comes in as a recording.

</Steps>

With **USB**, Prisme uses all the channels of the card: its four inputs for two turntables, its four outputs for an external mixer or for the master and the headphones. See [Sound card and latency](/docs/start/audio/#android-aaudio-and-usb) and [Setting up vinyl control](/docs/dvs/setup/).

### Controllers

A MIDI controller plugged in by USB is read through Android's own MIDI service, with the same profiles as on a computer. This has not been tried on a device yet: tell us how it goes. See [Controllers](/docs/controllers/).

## With the screen off

While a deck plays, Prisme keeps playing with the screen locked or another app in front, and shows a player on the lock screen and in the notifications: the track heard, its cover, and a key to play or pause each deck.

For a gig, turn off **Settings** > **DJ** > **Controls on the lock screen**: nothing in a pocket can then stop a deck.
