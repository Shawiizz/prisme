Prisme records your set to a WAV file and writes its tracklist next to it when you stop, with the
time each track came in. It records the [software mixer](/docs/mixer/mixer/)'s master, or with a
hardware mixer, what comes back into your sound card from the mixer's record output.

## Record

<Steps>

1. Click **Rec** in the title bar. It is greyed out while the audio is stopped.

2. Choose what to record:
   - **Master**: what the software mixer plays (only offered with the software mixer).
   - **Inputs 1-2**, **Inputs 3-4**…: what comes into that input pair of your sound card. Plug your
     hardware mixer's record output there.

3. The key turns red and counts the time. Mix as usual.

4. Click it again to stop (**Stop recording**). Prisme finishes the file and writes the tracklist.

</Steps>

Hover over **Rec** afterwards to see where the last recording went, how long it lasted and how many
tracks it holds.

On a phone, **Rec** at the top records the master straight away (or inputs 1-2 with an external
mixer). From a controller or the command palette, **Record the mix** starts and stops a recording of
the master.

<Aside type="caution">
**Record the mix** from a controller or the palette always records the software mixer's master.
With an external mixer, start the recording from **Rec** and choose your mixer's inputs instead.
</Aside>

## What is recorded

**Master** is the software mixer's master after the master level and the
[master limiter](/docs/mixer/mixer/#master-limiter), the open microphone and the aux line included.
It is taken before the balance, so **BAL** does not change the recording, and the booth level does
not either. The headphones and the [preview](/docs/mixer/headphones/#the-preview-player) are never
recorded.

**Inputs** record exactly what reaches that pair, at the level your mixer sends. Set your mixer's
record or booth output so its loudest peaks stay below full scale: Prisme does not limit it.

<Aside type="caution">
Do not pick the input pair a turntable is plugged into: you would record its timecode tone. The
turntables use inputs 1-2 for deck A and 3-4 for deck B.
</Aside>

## The file

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Format | WAV, stereo, 24-bit, at the sample rate the audio runs at |
| Folder | `Prisme Recordings` in your Documents folder, out of your music folder so it does not end up in the library |
| Name | The date and time it started: `Prisme 2026-10-11 21h30m05.wav` |

The file is kept up to date every second while it records, so a crash or a power cut loses a second
at most.

A WAV file holds about 4 GB, a little over four hours at 48 kHz. A longer set goes on in a second file,
`… 2.wav`, then `… 3.wav`. If the audio restarts during a recording (you change the sound card or the
buffer, or the device is unplugged and comes back), the recording goes on in the next file too.

If the disk cannot keep up, Prisme drops what does not fit rather than holding up the sound; the
**Rec** key's tooltip then says how many seconds were lost (**disk too slow**).

## The tracklist

When you stop, Prisme writes two files next to the recording, with the same name:

- a `.txt` file, one line per track: `0:12:40 Artist - Title`;
- a `.cue` sheet, which audio players and editors use to split the recording into its tracks.

The tracklist comes from the set's history. A track counts as played once it has been heard for 30
seconds on a deck, at a playing speed, and its time is when it started being heard. It gets the
artist and title from the track's tags, or its file name.

<Aside type="tip">
Start recording before the first track plays: a track that was already playing when you pressed
**Rec** is left out of the tracklist.
</Aside>

A track counts as heard even with its fader down, for instance while you cue it in the headphones
for 30 seconds: edit the `.txt` or `.cue` if that happens. With a recording split over several files,
only the `.txt` tracklist is written, as a CUE sheet describes a single file.
