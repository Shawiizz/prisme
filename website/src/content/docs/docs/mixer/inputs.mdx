The [software mixer](/docs/mixer/mixer/) can take two live sources from your sound card, a
**microphone** and an **aux line** (a phone, a CD player, a second mixer), and mix them into the
master. It can also send the master to **booth monitors** on an output pair of their own, at their
own level.

<Aside type="note">
All three need the software mixer. With an external mixer, plug the microphone, the aux source and
the booth into your mixer instead.
</Aside>

## Which inputs are free

Prisme keeps an input pair for each deck's turntable: inputs 1-2 for deck A, 3-4 for deck B, and so
on as far as the device goes. A microphone or an aux line can only use the inputs after those. On a
DJ interface with four inputs and two decks, there are none left; with six inputs, 5-6 are free.

If every input is taken, the settings say **The turntables take every input of this device.**, and
the choice is greyed out.

## Microphone

<Steps>

1. Plug the microphone into a free input of your sound card.

2. In **Settings** > **Mixing** > **Microphone**, choose its input: a single input (**Input 5**),
   heard in the middle of the stereo, or a pair (**Inputs 5-6**).

3. Close the settings. A **MIC** key now sits at the end of the crossfader, and the mixer's menu
   (the **⋯** key beside it) has a **Microphone** row.

4. Press **MIC** to open the microphone into the master. Press it again to close it.

</Steps>

The **Microphone** row of the mixer's menu holds:

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Meter | The microphone's level, after its **LEVEL** knob, even while it is closed. |
| **ON** | Opens the microphone into the master, like the **MIC** key. |
| **TALK** | Talkover on or off (see below). |
| Headphones key | Hears the microphone in the headphones, open or not, to check it before you talk. |
| **LEVEL** | Silent fully left, 0 dB at three quarters of the way, +12 dB fully right. |

The microphone is closed and out of the headphones each time Prisme starts, so it never comes on by
surprise. Its input and its level are kept.

Your voice goes in and out of the sound card through Prisme, so it reaches the speakers as late as
that round trip. Keep the buffer small (see [Audio setup](/docs/start/audio/)) if you talk a lot over the music. You do not
hear the microphone in the master of your headphones, only when you cue it.

### Talkover

Talkover turns the music down while the microphone is open, and back up when you close it, so you
can talk over the music without reaching for a fader.

1. In the mixer's menu, press **TALK** on the **Microphone** row. Its tooltip on the **MIC** key
   then reads **Microphone on / off: the music ducks while it is on**.
2. Choose how far the music goes down in **Settings** > **Mixing** > **Talkover**: **−6 dB**,
   **−12 dB**, **−18 dB** (the default) or **−24 dB**.

The music ducks in a tenth of a second when you open the microphone and comes back over about half a
second when you close it. Everything but the microphone ducks: the decks and the aux line. Talkover
stays on from one session to the next.

## Aux line

The aux line brings a stereo source into the master: a phone or a laptop for a guest DJ, a CD player,
the output of another mixer.

1. Plug the source into a free input pair.
2. In **Settings** > **Mixing** > **Aux line**, choose the pair (**Inputs 5-6**…). The aux line
   takes pairs only.
3. Open the mixer's menu: the **Aux line** row has its meter, **ON**, a headphones key and **LEVEL**,
   as the microphone's.

Press **ON** to bring it into the master. The aux line goes into the master like a deck: it is
limited with it, recorded with it, heard in the booth, and ducked under the microphone when talkover
is on. It is not on the crossfader.

If the microphone and the aux line need the same inputs, each list only offers what the other leaves.
A source set on inputs the current device does not have shows **Not on this device.** and comes
back when you plug that device in again.

## Booth output

Booth monitors play the master on a pair of outputs of their own, at a level you set apart from the
room's.

<Steps>

1. Plug the booth monitors into a free output pair of your sound card.

2. In **Settings** > **Mixing** > **Booth**, choose that pair (**Outputs 5-6**…). Outputs 1-2 are
   the master's and the headphones' pair is theirs: neither is offered. If you move the headphones
   onto the booth's pair, the headphones win: the booth's choice shows as **Outputs 3-4: the
   headphones**, greyed out, and the setting says **Not heard: the headphones are on outputs
   3-4.** Choose another pair for the booth. A pair the current device does not have shows
   **Not on this device.**

3. Set the level with the **BOOTH** knob, on the **Master** row of the mixer's menu. It goes from
   silence to 0 dB, the master's own level.

</Steps>

The booth gets the master after the master limiter, without the **BAL** balance: you can lean the
room's speakers to one side and keep the booth centred. The **Booth** setting only appears when the
device has an output pair beyond the master's.

## From a controller

The mixer actions for these sources are **Microphone level**, **Microphone on / off**, **Microphone
in the headphones**, **Aux line level**, **Aux line on / off**, **Aux line in the headphones**,
**Talkover: the music ducks under the microphone** and **Booth level**. See
[all actions](/docs/controllers/actions/).
