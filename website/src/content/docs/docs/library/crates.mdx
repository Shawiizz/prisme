Folders are how your files are stored; crates are how you dig through them for a set. A crate
only points at tracks: adding a track to a crate, removing it or deleting the crate never moves
or deletes a file. The same track can sit in as many crates as you like.

## Crates

<Steps>

1. In the side panel of the library, click **+** next to **Crates** (**New crate**).

2. Type a name and press <kbd>Enter</kbd> (<kbd>Esc</kbd> to give up). The new crate opens,
   empty.

3. Add tracks to it in any of these ways:
   - drag tracks from any list onto the crate;
   - right-click a track, **Add to a crate**, then the crate;
   - select several tracks and click **Crate** in the bar at the bottom of the list.

</Steps>

The number beside each crate is how many tracks it holds.

| To | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Rename a crate | Double-click it, type, <kbd>Enter</kbd> |
| Take tracks out | Open the crate, select them, press <kbd>Delete</kbd> or right-click › **Remove from the crate** |
| Delete a crate | Click the **×** at the end of its row, then **Delete** to confirm; <kbd>Esc</kbd>, a click elsewhere or a few seconds put the **×** back. Its tracks stay where they are |
| Export a crate as a playlist file | Point at it and click **M3U** at the end of its row. See [Exporting](/docs/library/export/) |

A crate's tracks can come from any folder. Crates imported from
[Serato or rekordbox](/docs/library/import/) are ordinary crates, and can be changed like
yours. Your crates, the prepare list and the sets also go to rekordbox, and from there to CDJs:
see [Exporting to rekordbox and playlists](/docs/library/export/).

## Smart crates

A smart crate fills itself: you give it rules, and it holds every analysed track of the library
that matches them, today and as you add music.

<Steps>

1. Click **+ Smart** next to **Crates**.

2. Give it a **Name**.

3. Choose **Tracks matching** **all of these rules** or **any of these rules**.

4. Set each rule: pick what it looks at, then its value. **Add a rule** adds another one, the
   **×** at the end of a rule removes it.

5. Click **Create**.

</Steps>

| Rule on | Matches | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Genre**, **Artist**, **Title**, **Album**, **Comment**, **File name** | The tag **contains** the text, upper or lower case alike | **Genre** contains `house` also finds *Deep House* and *Tech House* |
| BPM | The tempo is **from** one value **to** another, both included | from `122` to `126` |
| **Year** | The year tag is **from** one year **to** another, both included | from `1990` to `1999` |
| **Key** | The key **is one of** the Camelot codes you list, separated by spaces or commas | `8A 9A 8B` |

Leave **from** or **to** empty for no limit on that side: BPM from `140` with nothing after it
is everything at 140 BPM and above.

To change a smart crate's rules or name, double-click it. The crate shows with a funnel icon,
and you cannot drop tracks on it or take tracks out of it: change its rules instead.
Exported, as an M3U playlist or to rekordbox, it gives the tracks it picks at that moment.

<Aside type="note">
  The BPM and key rules use the tempo and key as corrected by you, if you corrected them (see
  [Tempo, beat grid and key](/docs/library/analysis/)). A track without a tempo or key never
  matches those rules, and a file that could not be decoded never matches any.
</Aside>

## The prepare list

**Prepare**, at the top of the side panel, is your queue for the next minutes of the set: the
tracks you plan to play, in the order you plan to play them.

- **Add tracks**: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> on the selection, drag tracks onto **Prepare**,
  right-click › **Add to the prepare list**, or **Prepare** in the bar of a multiple
  selection. A track already in the list stays where it is.
- **Reorder**: open the list and drag rows up or down; a line shows where they will land. Drop
  them below the last row to put them at the end. This works while the list is sorted by its
  `#` column, first to last.
- **Take out**: <kbd>Delete</kbd>, or right-click › **Remove from the prepare list**.
- **Export**: **M3U** at the end of its row writes it as a playlist file, in its order.

A track **leaves the list by itself once it has played for 30 seconds** on any deck, so the list
always starts with what is still to come. The list is kept when you quit Prisme.

The [Auto DJ](/docs/mixer/auto-dj/) plays this list on its own, each track mixed into the next.

## History

Each time you start Prisme and play tracks, a new set appears under **History**, named after the
day and time it started (`Sat 10 Oct, 21:30`), with the number of tracks played.

- A track is listed once it has been heard for **30 seconds** on a deck, at least at half speed:
  a quick preview or a mistake does not count. A track played twice is listed twice.
- Open a set to see its tracks in the order you played them, with the usual columns.
- **CSV** on a set's row exports its tracklist: number, start time, deck, artist, title, album,
  BPM, key and file. On Windows the file uses the separators of your regional settings, so
  Excel opens it straight away.
- **M3U** on a set's row exports it as a playlist file, to play it again elsewhere. See
  [Exporting](/docs/library/export/).
- The **×** on a set's row, then **Delete**, removes it from the history.

The tracks of your last set, if it started less than 12 hours ago, are dimmed in every list of
the library: a quick way to avoid playing a track twice in a night. They also go to the end
when you sort by how well tracks follow a deck (see
[Browsing and searching](/docs/library/browsing/#compare-with-a-deck)).

<Aside type="tip">
  Recording your mix also writes its tracklist next to the recording, as text and as a CUE
  sheet, from this history. See
  [Recording a mix](/docs/mixer/recording/).
</Aside>

## Duplicates

**Duplicates**, under **Prepare**, lists the tracks that look like the same song, grouped one
after the other. Two tracks are the same song when they have the same artist and title tags, or,
for tracks without both, the same file name; case, spaces and punctuation are ignored. So
*Strobe* by *deadmau5* in `strobe.mp3` and *strobe!* by *Deadmau5* in `Strobe (copy).flac` are
grouped together.

Compare their columns (BPM, plays, rating; hover a row to see its file) and keep the one you
want. Prisme does not delete files: remove the copies yourself in your file manager. Only analysed tracks are
compared.
