Prisme reads your system's **Music** folder, or another one you choose, with everything under
it, and any other folder you add: a USB key, an external drive, a folder of sets. There is
nothing to import and no list to build: copy tracks into one of those folders and they appear in
the library, then get their tempo, beat grid and key in the background.

## Where your music goes

| System  | The music folder                                                                  |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Windows | **Music**, wherever Windows keeps it: moved to another drive, a NAS or OneDrive works |
| macOS   | **Music** in your home folder                                                     |
| Linux   | Your desktop's music folder, usually `~/Music`                                    |
| Android | The shared **Music** folder of the phone                                          |

Sub-folders are fine, as deep as you like: they show in the side panel of the library, under
their folder, and the analysis walks through all of them.

<Aside type="tip">
  On Windows, if your collection lives on another drive, you can move the Music folder itself
  there: right-click **Music** in the Explorer, **Properties**, **Location** tab. Prisme follows
  wherever Windows says it is.
</Aside>

## Several folders

The library reads as many folders as you like, on a computer as on an Android phone. Each one
is the top of a tree under **Folders** in the side panel: its name, where it is beneath, and a
**Main** tag on the main one, the music folder of the settings (see below). The arrow beside a
folder shows the folders in it, a click opens it in the track list. All their tracks are in the
**Library** view of the search, analysed like those of **Music**.

To add one, click **+ Add a folder** at the top of **Folders** and pick it in the system's
folder picker, or drag it from the file manager and drop it on **Folders**: the side panel says
it will add it while the folder is over it. Prisme opens it, watches it and analyses its
tracks.

On an Android phone, **+ Add a folder** is at the top of the folders, in the lists of the
library (see [Phone and tablet](/docs/interface/phone/)). It rises with the places of the phone:
**Phone**, its own storage, then each SD card or USB drive plugged in. Tap your way down to the
folder, the arrow at the top going back up, and tap **Add this folder**: the **Download**
folder, say, or a folder of sets copied from a computer. Hidden folders and Android's own
**Android** folder are not offered.

Hover over a folder at the top of its tree and click **…** (on a phone, tap it):

- **Open in the file manager** shows it in the Explorer, the Finder or your file manager (on a
  computer);
- **Rescan** looks for new and changed files, as the toolbar's button does;
- **Remove from Prisme** takes it out of the library once you confirm: its tracks leave the
  library, the files stay where they are. The main folder stays; to change it, see below.

A folder on a USB key or a drive that is unplugged stays in the list, dimmed and marked
**unplugged**, its tracks kept: plug the drive back and it is there again, as it was.

A folder inside one of yours is already in the library, and a folder holding one of yours
cannot be added: Prisme says which folder is in the way. On an iPhone or an iPad, the library
reads Prisme's own documents only.

## Tracks from elsewhere

A file you drag from the file manager onto a deck plays even if it is in none of your folders:
Prisme analyses it as it loads and keeps it in the library, with its cues and corrections. Such
tracks gather under **Elsewhere**, after your folders in the side panel, with their number; the
row is there only while there are some. A click lists them, a **Folder** column saying where
each one is. In the other lists, the **Library** view of the search among them, a dashed folder
before the title tells them apart: hover over it to see their folder.

Right-click one (on a phone, tap it):

- **Show in the file manager** selects the file in the Explorer, the Finder or your file manager
  (on a computer);
- **Add “Downloads” to your folders**, named after its folder, makes that folder one of yours:
  it opens, the track in it with everything around it, and the track leaves **Elsewhere**. A
  folder holding one of yours cannot be added: the menu says which;
- **Remove from Prisme** takes it out of the library once you confirm; the file stays where it
  is. Its cues, corrections and crate entries are kept, as for a deleted file: load it again
  and it gets them back.

Several tracks selected together (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd> clicks) are removed
together, and their folder added if they share it.

A track in one of your folders on a drive that is unplugged is not elsewhere: it stays under
its folder, waiting for the drive.

## Choosing another folder

On a computer, **Settings** › **Library** › **Music folder** shows the main folder Prisme reads.
**Choose…** picks another one (a folder on an external drive or a NAS, say), **Reset** goes
back to the system's **Music** folder. The choice is kept for the next start.

The new folder is watched and scanned at once. The tracks of the folder you leave go out of the
library, as deleted files do:

- those you copied or moved into the new folder keep everything they had (analysis, cues, grid
  and key corrections, crates, history) without being analysed again;
- the others are forgotten, but for their cues, corrections and crate entries: if you choose
  their folder again, they get them back, and are analysed again.

On a phone the music folder is the system's, and cannot be changed.

What is skipped:

- hidden files and folders, and those whose name starts with a dot;
- system folders such as the recycle bin;
- symbolic links to folders: put the music itself in the folder, not a link to it;
- files that a cloud folder (OneDrive and the like) keeps online only. Reading them would
  download them, so they wait until you make them available on the device.

## Supported formats

MP3, AAC and ALAC (`.m4a`, `.mp4`, `.aac`), FLAC, Ogg Vorbis (`.ogg`, `.oga`), WAV and AIFF
(`.aif`, `.aiff`). Prisme recognises them by their extension; other files are left out of the
lists.

## The first analysis

The first time Prisme starts, it scans the music folder and analyses every track it finds:
tempo and beat grid, musical key, waveform, cover. The library's toolbar shows how far it is:
**Looking for new files…**, then **Analysing 120 / 3400**.

- You can browse, search and play while it runs. A track whose analysis is not done yet shows
  `···` in the BPM column; its row fills in as soon as the result comes.
- The analysis runs at low priority, on up to half of the processor's cores (four at most),
  so playback is never starved.
- By default it **pauses while a deck plays**: the status turns to **Analysis paused**, and it
  goes on once the decks stop. A track you load is analysed on the spot anyway, paused or not.
  You can let the analysis run during a set with the setting below.
- Files that cannot be decoded are counted as **unreadable** in the toolbar and show `—` in the
  BPM column. Try opening them in another player: they are usually damaged or not really the
  format their extension says.

Results are stored in Prisme's library database, not next to your music. A track is analysed
once; it is analysed again only if the file changes (its size or modification time) or if a new
version of Prisme improves the analysis.

See [Tempo, beat grid and key](/docs/library/analysis/) for what the analysis finds and how to
correct it.

## New tracks and rescanning

Prisme watches your music folders. When you copy an album in, it waits for the copy to settle
(about a second and a half of quiet), then analyses the new and changed files. The folder you
have open in the library updates by itself.

If you want to be sure, click **Rescan** in the library's toolbar (**Analyse new and changed
files**). It looks through all your folders; when nothing is left to do it says **Library up
to date**.

If a music folder sits on a drive or a NAS that goes away, Prisme looks for it again every
half minute and scans it once it is back.

## Moved, renamed or retagged files

Reorganising your folders does not lose anything. What Prisme knows of a track (analysis, cues,
beat grid and key corrections, colour, rating, crates, history, prepare list) follows the file
when:

- you move or rename a file or a whole folder inside the music folder;
- you rename a file and change its tags: Prisme recognises it by its audio;
- a USB drive comes back under another drive letter;
- you move a file into the music folder from elsewhere on the computer.

A file you delete is forgotten at the next scan, but its cues, corrections and crate entries
are kept: if the same audio comes back later, it gets them again. Tracks on a drive that is
unplugged are left as they are, not forgotten.

## Writing tempo and key into your files

So that Serato, rekordbox and other software find them, Prisme can write the tempo and the key
it found into the tags of your files. These settings are under **Settings** › **Library**:

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Write the tempo and key into the tags of the files** | Writes the BPM and the key (in musical notation, `Am`, `F#`) of each analysed track, and again when you correct its grid or key | On on a computer, off on a phone |
| **Replace those another program wrote** | Shown when the first is on. A tempo or key that Mixed In Key or another program put in a file is kept unless this is on | Off |
| **Pause the analysis of the library while a deck plays** | Keeps the processor and the disk for the sound during a set; a track copied meanwhile is analysed once the decks stop, or when it is loaded | On |

Tags are written in MP3, WAV, FLAC, Ogg and M4A/MP4 files. Prisme writes them in the
background, one file at a time, and only the fields it needs: the Serato cues and grids stored in your tags
are left untouched. A file Prisme just tagged is not mistaken for a changed file and is not
analysed again.

The genre is different: you set it by hand from the track menu, and it is always written into
the file (see [Browsing and searching](/docs/library/browsing/#the-track-menu)). Colours and
ratings stay in Prisme's library.

<Aside type="caution">
  A tag write works on a copy beside the file, named like `.track.mp3.prisme-tags`, then puts it
  in place. If Prisme is cut off in the middle, that hidden copy may stay; the next scan removes
  it. Your file itself is left as it was.
</Aside>

## When something looks wrong

- **A track is missing from the list.** Check its extension is in the list above, that it is
  not hidden, and that it is really inside one of your folders (not behind a link). Then
  **Rescan**.
- **The analysis never moves.** It is probably paused because a deck is playing. Stop the decks,
  or turn off **Pause the analysis of the library while a deck plays**.
- **A red `library not saved` chip in the title bar.** The library database could not be opened:
  Prisme still works, but cues, corrections and crates changed now are lost when you quit. See
  [Troubleshooting](/docs/help/troubleshooting/).

Your tracks are in: next, [browse and search them](/docs/library/browsing/), or bring your cues
and crates over from [Serato or rekordbox](/docs/library/import/).
